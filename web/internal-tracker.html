    Playbook Reference

    The full sourcing strategy, kept here for reference. The living version is in the repo.

    Site screen — before anyone gets a call

    Kill criteria: within ~1–2 mi of 161/345 kV (or ~3 mi of a substation with room); 300–800+ contiguous acres across ≤6–8 owners; core footprint out of the 100-yr floodplain, slopes under ~5%; a plausible zoning path (CUP or rezone); hard-surface road access.

    Scoring preferences: lower-CSR2 ground (50s–60s — cheaper, and owners more open to alternatives; sort the parcel universe by CSR2 ascending); absentee or aging ownership, estates, trusts; existing disturbance (closed ethanol plants, gravel pits, rail-served ground); water and fiber nearby; a county already comfortable with energy projects.

    Pre-build per cluster: a one-pager — parcel map with owners color-coded, acreage, CSR2, assessed value, transmission distance, floodplain overlay, and the assemblage sequence (anchor / optional / risk).

    The 19 sourcing channels

    Tier 1 — data-driven (free, do first):

    1. County GIS parcel universe — Beacon / Vanguard portals + transmission overlay → every parcel in the buffer with owner and tax mailing address.
    2. Absentee-owner extraction — out-of-county tax addresses; weakest emotional ties, highest cold-outreach yield.
    3. Estate / trust / life-event signals — probate filings, trust-owned parcels, no-saled auctions (owner already decided to sell), expiring CRP contracts.
    4. Tax-stress signals — treasurer's delinquent lists; owners who might welcome a clean exit.

    Tier 2 — relationships (the real Iowa land market):

    1. Farm management companies — Hertz, Peoples Company, Farmers National, Stalcup, bank trust departments; one manager = dozens of owners.
    2. Brokers retained as buyer's scouts — retainer + success fee to originate, not to check listings.
    3. Auction houses — Sullivan, Steffes; pre-auction private deals.
    4. Ag lenders & trust officers — can't share client info, can pass our one-pager along.
    5. County-seat estate attorneys — credibility + they know every family situation.
    6. Tenants as intermediaries — over half of Iowa farmland is rented; a tenant with upside becomes an advocate.

    Tier 3 — community & institutional:

    1. County EDC offices — call before calling landowners; they know who's sale-curious.
    2. Utilities & RECs — MidAmerican, Alliant, ITC Midwest siting teams; parcels near their own substations.
    3. Expired wind/solar options — county recorder search → a literal list of energy-friendly owners.
    4. Distressed industrial sites — closed ethanol plants, idle rail-served parks; IEDA certified sites list.

    Tier 4 — creative plays:

    1. Section-line letters — 30–80 per cluster, personalized with the legal description; 5–10× generic mail response.
    2. Plat book + coffee shop circuit — two days in county; within a week sellers call you. Only works if the story is true.
    3. "Keep farming it" offers — option payment now, rent-free farming until construction.
    4. Land swaps & 1031s — capital-gains fear kills more deals than price; lead with the structure.
    5. Township trustees & Farm Bureau officers — the informal mayors; heads-up calls end with "you ought to talk to ___."
    Assemblage discipline

    Options, not purchases: exclusive 12–36 month options, non-refundable payment credited at closing; record a memorandum (not the option — keeps price private); rent-free farming until exercise; price fixed up front, never open at exercise.

    Iowa-specific (local counsel from day one): Iowa Code ch. 9H corporate farmland restrictions — entity structure and option wording matter before the first signature. Farm tenancies auto-renew unless terminated by Sept 1. Keep a 1031 facilitator ready.

    Sequencing: anchor parcel first; design ≥2 viable configurations per cluster (alternates kill holdout leverage); uniform per-acre pricing framework — owners compare numbers at church; contingent options for the tail; pre-set walk-away numbers before the first call.

    Confidentiality, the honest version: neutral-name project LLC (standard, legal); everyone tells the same story — evaluating feasibility of a potential energy campus, battery storage or similar; if asked who's behind it: "we can't share who we're working with at this stage" — never a denial. NDAs with intermediaries; you can't keep the project's existence quiet, so don't build a plan that needs that.

    Scripts — call, voicemail, letter, pitches

    Ground rules: one story, told the same by everyone. Discreet ≠ deceptive — don't name the end user, do answer honestly, never deny something true. Under-promise (it's a feasibility study). One letter, one voicemail, one follow-up; "don't call again" gets logged and honored.

    Cold call (after letter):

    "Hi, is this [owner]? My name's [name] — I sent you a letter last week about your farm in Section [X] of [township] Township. Do you have two minutes? The short version: I'm working with a group studying whether a potential energy campus — battery storage, that kind of energy infrastructure — could work in [county] County. Your ground sits in an area that could fit, mostly because of where it is relative to the power lines. I want to be straight with you — this is early, and I'm not calling to pressure anybody. One question: if the numbers were right, is selling or optioning some or all of that ground something you'd ever entertain, or is it just not on the table?"

    If open: coffee this week, walk through how an option works — "you'd farm it just like normal unless the project actually goes." If no: thank them, ask for referrals — "no" calls produce referrals constantly. If hostile: off the list, no argument; the neighbors are watching how we behave.

    Voicemail (≤20 s):

    "Hi [name], this is [name] — I mailed you a letter about your farmland in [township] Township. We're studying a potential energy project in the county and had a quick question about your ground. No pressure either way — [number]. Thanks."

    First-touch letter: personalized with the legal description; states the real story (early-stage feasibility, energy campus / battery storage); offers option structures where they keep farming; invites a "not interested" reply; promises one phone follow-up. Hand-signed, stamped not metered, local return address, one page, no gloss.

    Broker / farm-manager pitch: "[300–800]+ contiguous acres in [counties], energy-campus class. Nothing listed works, so we're originating off-market. Full commission, options so sellers keep farming, clean closes. NDA and we'll show you the screened areas."

    EDC / township heads-up: "Wanted you to hear it from us before any landowner calls. Feasibility study, very early, nothing committed — and if it moves forward, everything goes through your public process."

    Objections:

    They sayWe say
    "What's this really for?""Can't name who we're working with yet — normal at this stage — but the use is what I said. If it proceeds it all becomes public in permitting."
    "Not selling the farm.""Would an option interest you? Paid now, keep farming, sale only if the project's real — with years of notice."
    "Capital gains would kill me.""1031 facilitators — roll into replacement ground tax-deferred. Talk to your accountant; we structure around it."
    "My tenant rents it.""Built in — tenancy stays; under an option they farm until construction, years out if ever."
    "What's it worth to you?""Recent county sales and CSR2, at a premium — same framework for every owner, so nobody loses by talking first. Real number on paper after I see the ground."
    "Neighbors will hate it.""Maybe some — that's why we're with the county and townships early, telling one true story. Nothing happens outside the public process."
    Data sources (all free)
    • Ownership: Beacon (beacon.schneidercorp.com) / Vanguard assessor portals; county GIS shapefiles; geodata.iowa.gov; Farm & Home plat books.
    • Soil: CSR2 via assessor portals, ISU ISPAID, AcreValue free tier; USDA Web Soil Survey.
    • Energy/infrastructure: HIFLD / EIA Energy Atlas (transmission by kV, substations); MISO interconnection queue (withdrawn projects = pre-optioned landowners); Iowa Utilities Commission territory maps; FEMA flood layer; Iowa DOT rail GIS.
    • Motivation signals: Iowa Courts Online probate search; county recorder (expired wind/solar easements); treasurer delinquent lists; Sullivan/Steffes auction results (flag no-sales); USDA CRP expirations.
    • Pricing & intel: ISU Extension Farmland Value Survey; auction comps adjusted by CSR2; county EDC + IEDA certified sites; supervisors' meeting minutes (search prior wind/solar/BESS items).
    Four-week sequence

    Weeks 1–2: parcel universe, screen clusters, rank owners. Week 2: retain local attorney + one broker/farm-manager channel. Week 3: EDC + utility conversations; letters drop for cluster #1. Week 4+: calls follow letters by 7–10 days; coffee-shop circuit same window; iterate cluster by cluster.
