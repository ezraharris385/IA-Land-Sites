You did not run out of sites. You ran out of listings — and public listings systematically select for the wrong ground.

Every 500+ acre tract in your file sits in a landform region Iowa geologists classify as dissected: Southern Iowa Drift Plain, cut by streams, which is precisely why the tracts are big, cheap and available — nobody wants to farm them. Iowa's flat, buildable 500-acre blocks are prime tiled row-crop ground worth $12–16k/acre. That land is never listed in big blocks. It is held, farmed, and sold by auction or handshake. To find it you stop searching for sites and start searching for ground.

PLATE 04

Drop the solo-site constraint

You said it yourself: "this is the very best I could find for solo sites." That constraint is the expensive one. Iowa was surveyed on the Public Land Survey grid — a section is 640 acres, a quarter-section 160, and gravel roads run the section lines. Your entire acreage target fits inside a single section. Nobody assembles hyperscale campuses from one listing: Meta at Altoona is 520 acres, QTS at Cedar Rapids 612, Google's proposed Palo site 545 — all assembled or park-based.

Average Iowa farm is 345 acres, median 130 (29,978,165 farmland acres across 86,911 farms, USDA 2022). Ownership is more fragmented than operation — most large operators rent — so the acres-per-owner slider defaults below the average deliberately. Plate 11 gives you the real median by county.

My judgment, not fact.

  • Screen scores and all five sub-scores are mine, derived from the fields in your two workbooks. Where your sheet was blank I scored conservatively. Every sub-score is visible in Plate 01 so you can overwrite it — treat them as a starting rank order, not a valuation.
  • Hydrology haircuts are estimates keyed to your own notes, not to delineations. A wetland determination and a FEMA LOMR can move any of them by hundreds of acres in either direction.
  • County scores in Plate 06 weight landform and ordinance status. They are a desk screen for where to point a GIS query — not a substitute for calling the zoning administrator, which you should do before every site visit.
  • The Plate 03 funnel is a sizing argument, not a count. Its purpose is to establish the order of magnitude of what you have not looked at. Its sliders are yours to set.
  • Landform assignments are county-level. Several Iowa counties straddle two regions — Dallas, Polk, Greene, Butler and Woodbury among them — so confirm at township level before you rely on it.
  • Counties without countywide zoning are the single highest-leverage screen on this sheet and no published list exists — I looked hard, including the ISU Extension zoning guide, which covers cities only. What is documented: roughly half of Iowa's 99 counties have adopted county zoning, so the unzoned pool is large; Iowa County is unzoned outside two-mile city rings and the Amana district; Washington County rescinded its ordinance in 2013; Chickasaw is reported unzoned; and your own Lohrville listing records no zoning in Calhoun. Everything else is a phone call. Plate 13 has the script.
  • Owners per section is a floor, not a count. It divides 640 by the county median operation size. Large operators farm rented ground across several owners, so true ownership is more fragmented and the real signature count is higher. Calibrate the multiplier on your first county's parcel dissolve before trusting it statewide.
  • The county score in Plate 11 weights landform 30, assemblage 28, ordinance 27, pool depth 15. Those weights are my judgment. The underlying farm data is USDA's; the landform and ordinance inputs are mine.
  • Contacts are publicly listed business contacts for people whose role is to field exactly this enquiry. Verify before you dial — staff turn over, and the moratorium list moved nine times in 2026.
  • The HF 976 city-population test needs tax counsel. The statute keys the 15-versus-10-year energy exemption to the population of the city where the centre is located. How that applies to a site in unincorporated county territory with no city of record is exactly the question to put to counsel before you choose between annexation and staying in the county.
