# Assemblage Strategy — Controlling Multiple Parcels Without Blowing Up the Price

Assembling 2–6 contiguous ownerships is the most likely path to a viable
site. It's also where deals die: one leak, one greedy holdout, one neighbor
feud, and pricing runs away. This is the discipline.

## Structure: options, not purchases

- **Exclusive option to purchase** (12–36 months, extendable) with a
  non-refundable option payment credited at closing. We control the ground
  through diligence and interconnection studies without owning farmland we
  may never use.
- **Record a memorandum of option**, not the option itself — puts the world
  on notice of our position without publishing price or terms.
- **Rent-free farming until exercise** (owner or their tenant keeps farming).
  This is often the emotional unlock — the land keeps looking like a farm
  and the tenant keeps their acres until the project is real.
- **Price certainty up front.** Fix the purchase price (or price per acre)
  in the option. Never leave price open to negotiation at exercise.

## Iowa-specific structural notes (get local counsel on all of these)

- **Iowa Code ch. 9H (corporate farmland ownership restrictions).** Iowa
  restricts most corporate entities from acquiring agricultural land. There
  are exemptions — including acquisition for purposes other than farming
  with development-timeline conditions, and options/easements are treated
  differently from fee acquisition — but the entity structure and the option
  wording need Iowa ag-land counsel from day one, not at closing.
- **Tenant notice.** Iowa farm tenancies auto-renew unless terminated by
  September 1 for the following crop year. If a parcel has a tenant, the
  termination calendar drives your closing calendar.
- **1031 exchanges.** Have a facilitator relationship ready; the ability to
  say "we can structure this so you roll into other ground tax-deferred" is
  a closing tool, not a nicety.

## Sequencing the owners

1. **Anchor first.** Identify the parcel without which no configuration
   works, and the owner most likely to transact (from the sourcing screen).
   Get the anchor under option before approaching anyone whose land is
   optional.
2. **Alternates create honesty.** Design at least two viable configurations
   per cluster. When an owner is genuinely optional, you can pay fairly but
   walk from a hold-up. When every parcel is essential, every owner can name
   their price — so avoid single-configuration clusters.
3. **Uniform, defensible pricing.** In a small township, owners will compare
   numbers at church. Set a consistent per-acre framework (e.g., a stated
   premium over recent county sales adjusted for CSR2) and hold it. The
   moment one owner gets a secret sweetener, everyone reopens.
4. **Contingent options for the tail.** Later options can be contingent on
   the assemblage completing — "this only closes if the project proceeds" —
   which is honest and protects us from owning orphan parcels.
5. **Holdout math.** Decide before the first call what each configuration
   supports as a walk-away number. A holdout can only extract value if we
   haven't built the alternative.

## Confidentiality — the honest version

- Use a project LLC with a neutral name for optioning. Standard practice,
  legal, and expected in site acquisition. The LLC is real and its purpose
  is real; we're not disclosing the end user, and if asked directly whether
  there's a larger company behind the project, the answer is "we're not able
  to share who we're working with at this stage" — not a denial.
- Every owner-facing person tells the same story: **evaluating the
  feasibility of a potential energy campus — battery storage or similar
  energy infrastructure.** Nobody improvises a different cover.
- NDAs with brokers, farm managers, and attorneys; owners get a
  confidentiality clause in the option (price/terms confidential — you
  cannot realistically keep the *existence* of the project quiet, so don't
  build a plan that depends on that).

## Risk register

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Price contagion after first deal leaks | Uniform pricing framework; move fast between anchor and neighbors |
| Holdout on essential parcel | Multi-configuration design; pre-set walk-away |
| County/community backlash | Early EDC + supervisor + township trustee contact; truthful story from day one |
| 9H entity problem discovered late | Iowa ag-land counsel before first option is signed |
| Tenant termination deadline missed | Sept 1 calendar item per tenanted parcel |
| Interconnection fails after options signed | Options (not purchases); staged option periods matching study timelines |
