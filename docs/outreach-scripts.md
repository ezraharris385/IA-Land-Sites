# Outreach Scripts & Templates

## Ground rules (read before any call)

- **The story is:** we're evaluating the area for a **potential energy
  campus — battery storage or similar energy infrastructure** — and looking
  for landowners who might consider selling or optioning ground. That is
  the whole story and everyone tells it the same way.
- **Discreet ≠ deceptive.** Don't name the end user; do answer honestly.
  If asked "is this for a data center / for [company]?" → *"I can't share
  who we're working with at this stage — what I can tell you is we're
  studying whether an energy campus is feasible here, and if it moves
  forward everything becomes public through the county process."* Never
  flatly deny something that's true, and never invent detail that isn't.
- **Under-promise.** It's a *feasibility* conversation. Don't imply a done
  deal, guaranteed price, or certain timeline.
- **We are buying, not selling** — these are purchase inquiries, not
  telemarketing — but still: one voicemail, one letter, one follow-up call.
  If someone says "not interested, don't call again," log it and honor it.
- **Log every touch** in `tracker/outreach-log.csv` the same day. Small
  townships compare notes; our consistency is our credibility.

## Cold call script (owner, after letter)

> "Hi, is this [owner name]? My name's [name] — I sent you a letter last
> week about your farm in Section [X] of [township] Township, wasn't sure
> if it reached you. Do you have two minutes?
>
> The short version: I'm working with a group that's studying whether a
> potential energy campus — battery storage, that kind of energy
> infrastructure — could work in [county] County. Your ground sits in an
> area we think could fit, mostly because of where it is relative to the
> power lines.
>
> I want to be straight with you — this is early, and I'm not calling to
> pressure anybody. I'm calling to ask one question: if the numbers were
> right, is selling or optioning some or all of that ground something
> you'd ever entertain, or is it just not on the table?"

**If open:** "Great — I'd rather do this in person than over the phone.
Could I buy you coffee this week and walk you through how an option works?
You'd farm it just like normal unless the project actually goes."

**If no:** "Completely understand, and I appreciate you telling me
straight. If anything ever changes, my number's on the letter. Can I ask —
anyone else in the area you think might want the call?" *(Always ask. "No"
calls produce referrals constantly.)*

**If hostile:** "Understood — I'll take you off our list. Thanks for your
time." *(Log it. Do not argue. Their neighbor is watching how we behave.)*

## First-touch letter (personalized, real signature, local return address)

> Dear [owner name],
>
> I'm writing about your farmland in Section [X], [township] Township
> ([approx. acres] acres). I represent a group evaluating whether a
> potential energy campus — battery storage and related energy
> infrastructure — could be feasible in [county] County. Your land is in
> an area that may fit because of its location relative to existing
> transmission lines.
>
> This is an early-stage study, not a done deal. But if you would ever
> consider selling or optioning some or all of this ground at a fair
> price — including structures where you or your tenant keep farming it
> unless and until a project actually proceeds — I would welcome a
> conversation. If selling isn't of interest, a reply saying so is
> genuinely helpful too, and we won't bother you again.
>
> I'll follow up by phone in the next week or two. You can also reach me
> directly at [phone] or [email].
>
> Respectfully,
> [name], [title]
> [project LLC name] · [local address]

**Details that raise response rates:** legal-description specificity,
hand-signed, stamped (not metered), local or in-state return address, and
one page only. No glossy anything.

## Broker / farm manager pitch (channel partners)

> "We're in the market for [300–800]+ contiguous acres in [counties] for a
> potential energy campus — battery storage class of project. Nothing
> listed works, so we're originating off-market. If you have clients who'd
> entertain an offer or an option — estates, absentee owners, anyone who's
> said 'for the right number' — we pay full commission, we use options so
> sellers keep farming until a project is real, and we close clean. Can we
> put you under a simple NDA and show you the areas we're screening?"

## County EDC / township trustee heads-up

> "Before we talk to any landowners in your area, we wanted you to hear
> from us directly. We're studying the feasibility of an energy campus —
> battery storage or similar — in [county] County. Very early, nothing
> committed. We'd value your read on which areas make sense, and we intend
> to do this the right way — publicly through your process if it moves
> forward."

## Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "What's this really for? Who's behind it?" | "Fair question. I can't name who we're working with yet — that's normal at this stage — but the use is what I said: an energy campus, battery storage or similar. If it proceeds, all of it becomes public in the county permitting process." |
| "I don't want to sell the farm." | "Understood. Would an option interest you? You get paid now, keep farming, and a sale only ever happens if the project is real — and you'd know years in advance." |
| "What about taxes? I'd get killed on capital gains." | "We work with 1031 exchange facilitators — many sellers roll into replacement farmland tax-deferred. Worth a conversation with your accountant; we can structure around it." |
| "My tenant has it rented." | "We build that in — the tenancy stays in place, and under an option your tenant farms it until construction, which is years out if ever." |
| "What's it worth to you?" | "We price off recent county sales and CSR2, at a premium — and we use the same framework with every owner, so nobody gets a worse deal for talking to us first. Let me put a real number on paper after I see the ground." |
| "The neighbors will hate this." | "Maybe some will — that's why we're talking to the county and townships early and telling everyone the same true story. Nothing happens outside the public permitting process." |
